# Shrink kiss-seo to what models miss

For a 5–100 page small-business or training site built as static HTML, SEO best practice in November 2026 is shorter than the industry says. **Google states that AI Overviews and AI Mode need "no additional requirements", no "AI text files" and "no special schema.org structured data"**, so "AI search" reduces to ordinary indexability plus clear, specific, first-hand writing. Rich results have narrowed: **FAQ (removed June 2026), HowTo (2023), the sitelinks search box (2024) and Course info (2025) are gone**, and the Course list carousel is the only education rich result left. Local visibility is decided mostly by the Google Business Profile and reviews, which a website build does not control. Against that, `kiss-seo` contains **nothing the research shows to be wrong**. It makes no character-limit, FAQ, HowTo, Course or llms.txt claims. Its problem is the one the 2026-10-31 fleet measurement found: with or without it, agents made "the same changes on all three sites, twice the same title word for word". Roughly half of its lines repeat what the engine's audit, `kiss-site-review` or another domain skill already says. The corrections that matter sit in the **engine and GUIDE, not the skill**. The sitemap stamps build time as every page's `lastmod`, and both Google and Bing discount that. GUIDE.md calls `llms.txt` "the file an answer engine reads before it crawls", which no platform documents and log studies contradict. The improvement is a shorter skill aimed at the dated, post-training facts and traps an agent gets wrong, with a with-and-without brief for each line.

## Best practice in 2026 is the basics, applied consistently

**Titles and descriptions have no length rule.** Google builds the title link from `<title>`, the main heading, `og:title` and other sources. It truncates "as needed, typically to fit the device width" and names no character count. Rewrites come from half-empty titles ("| Site Name"), obsolete dates, inaccuracy, near-identical boilerplate across a site, and pages with no clear main heading ([Google: title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link)). For the brand, Google advises "just your site name at the beginning or end of each <title> element". Snippets come "primarily" from page content. The meta description is used when it describes the page better than the content does, and "identical or similar descriptions on every page of a site aren't helpful" ([Google: snippets](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet)). The 60- and 160-character figures are tool folklore, so a uniqueness check is defensible and a length check is not.

**Canonical signals must all name one URL.** Google ranks redirects as "a strong signal", `rel=canonical` next and sitemap inclusion as "a weak signal". It asks for absolute canonical URLs, sitemaps listing only canonical URLs, and never canonicalising with robots.txt or `noindex` ([Google: consolidate duplicates](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls)). kiss already enforces this through `config.links.trailingSlash`, which feeds the canonical, `<loc>`, `{{link}}`, feed and alias targets from one function. **robots.txt controls crawling, not indexing.** A blocked page "can still appear in search results" without a description, and a `noindex` behind a `Disallow` is never seen ([Google: robots.txt intro](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/robots/intro); [robots meta tag](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/robots-meta-tag)). GUIDE.md § .robots() already states both rules correctly.

**Sitemaps: `lastmod` counts only when it is true.** Google uses `lastmod` only if it is "consistently and verifiably" accurate, and "ignores `<priority>` and `<changefreq>` values" ([Google: build a sitemap](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap)). Bing calls `lastmod` "a key signal" for recrawling in AI-era search, and trade coverage of the same guidance says not to set it to the generation time unless the page changed ([Bing Webmaster Blog, July 2025](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/July-2025/Keeping-Content-Discoverable-with-Sitemaps-in-AI-Powered-Search); [Search Engine Journal](https://beta.searchenginejournal.com/bing-recommends-lastmod-tags-for-ai-search-indexing/552642/)). IndexNow reaches Bing, Yandex, Naver, Seznam and others but **not Google** ([IndexNow FAQ](https://www.indexnow.org/faq)). It is a deploy-hook concern, not a build one.

**Headings, word count and keywords matter less than the folklore says.** Google's starter guide says heading order "doesn't matter", there is "no magical, ideal amount of headings", content length "alone doesn't matter", and keywords in the URL "have hardly any effect" ([Google SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)). One `<h1>` is still good practice, for accessibility and for title-link clarity, not for ranking. **Core Web Vitals (LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms, CLS ≤ 0.1)** are "used by our ranking systems", but relevance wins: Google will show "the most relevant content, even if the page experience is sub-par" ([Google: page experience](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/page-experience); [web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)). A lean static site passes almost by default.

**Content quality is now a Who/How/Why test.** Google's people-first page (updated 2026-10-05) asks whether it is "self-evident to your visitors who authored your content". It treats fabricated identities, such as AI headshots, invented names and false credentials, as deception. It asks for disclosure of AI involvement where a reader would wonder, and it lists "changing page dates without substantive updates" as a search-engine-first warning sign ([Google: creating helpful content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)). Google's generative-AI guidance says it is "critical to manually factcheck and review all AI-generated content". That review covers titles, meta descriptions, structured data and alt text, not only body copy ([Google: using generative AI content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)). The spam policy that bites a small site is **doorway abuse**: "pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page". Scaled content abuse covers generating "many pages without adding value for users", whether a human or an AI wrote them ([Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)). An agent asked to "add a page for every town we cover" is walking into exactly that policy.

### Structured data now pays only for a short list of types

Google's Search Gallery listed 25 features on 2026-10-16 ([Search Gallery](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/search-gallery)). Since 2023 Google has retired these:

- HowTo, September 2023.
- The sitelinks search box, November 2024.
- Six types in June 2025, including **Course Info** and **Learning Video**, with their docs removed on 2025-09-09 ([Google blog, June 2025](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/06/simplifying-search-results)).
- Practice problem, docs removed January 2026.
- **FAQ**, deprecated 2026-05-08 with its docs removed 2026-06-15 ([changelog](https://developers.google.com/search/updates)).

For a training provider, the **Course list** carousel survives. It needs **at least three courses** in `ItemList` plus Carousel markup, it is English-only, and it applies only to courses "with an explicit learning outcome, instructors, and enrolled students" ([Google: Course list](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/course)). The self-serving rule still holds: a business that "controls the reviews about itself" cannot earn star snippets on its `LocalBusiness` or `Organization` markup, embedded review widgets included ([Google: review snippet](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet)). Organization markup has no required properties and belongs on the home or About page ([Google: Organization](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/organization)). LocalBusiness needs only `name` and `address` and makes no ranking claim ([Google: LocalBusiness](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business)). Breadcrumb rich results have been **desktop-only since 2025-01-22**. Structured data is not a ranking factor: a structured-data manual action "doesn't affect how the page ranks" ([Google: structured data guidelines](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies)). Removed types do no harm if they are truthful. They simply earn nothing in Google.

### Local search is mostly decided off the website

Google's own ranking factors are relevance, distance and prominence. Prominence includes "how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have" ([GBP Help: local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)). The profile's name must be the real-world name, and keyword stuffing it "could result in the suspension" of the profile. A business that does not serve customers at its address must hide it, and can list up to 20 service areas within about two hours' drive ([GBP guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177); [service areas](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481)). The 2026 Whitespark expert survey, which is opinion and not measurement, puts "Dedicated page for each service" first for local organic results. It ranks "HTML NAP matching GBP NAP" only 15th for the map pack ([Whitespark LSRF 2026](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/)). In the UK, the DMCC Act has banned fake and concealed-incentive reviews since 6 April 2025, and CMA fines reach 10% of global turnover ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/no-more-faux-five-stars-the-dmcc-act-bans-fake-reviews)). That is a legal constraint on how a site shows testimonials, not only an SEO one. The site's share of this work is narrow: one page per real service, the profile's name, phone and hours repeated as crawlable text, and area pages only where the place has genuinely distinct content.

## AI search rewards indexable HTML, not llms.txt

**Google's AI features add no requirements.** A page must be "indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet". `nosnippet` and `max-snippet` are the only levers that reduce AI use, and they reduce classic snippets too ([Google: AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)). Google-Extended governs Gemini training and grounding and "does not impact a site's inclusion in Google Search" ([Google common crawlers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/google-common-crawlers)).

**Other vendors split training crawlers from search crawlers, and the split is where small sites lose visibility by accident.**

- OpenAI: disallowing `GPTBot` opts out of training, while `OAI-SearchBot` is what surfaces sites in ChatGPT search ([OpenAI crawlers](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots)).
- Anthropic: it separates `ClaudeBot` (training) from `Claude-SearchBot` (search indexing) and `Claude-User` ([Anthropic support](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8896518-does-anthropic-crawl-data-from-the-web-and-how-can-site-owners-block-the-crawler)).
- Edge networks: Cloudflare began blocking AI crawlers by default for new customers on 2025-07-01 ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/07/01/1119498/cloudflare-will-now-by-default-block-ai-bots-from-crawling-its-clients-websites/)). From 2026-09-15 its defaults also block "mixed-use" crawlers on pages that host ads, including for existing free customers ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/01/cloudflares-new-policy-pushes-ai-companies-to-pay-for-publishers-content/)). The robots.txt kiss writes is therefore not the whole answer for a site behind a CDN.

AI crawlers other than Googlebot did not execute JavaScript in the only systematic study (Vercel/MERJ, December 2024), so complete server-delivered HTML is the safe shape. kiss produces that by construction ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/blog/the-rise-of-the-ai-crawler)).

**llms.txt has no evidence of a search benefit.** SE Ranking's study of about 300,000 domains (May 2026) found **no correlation** between llms.txt and AI citations, and removing the variable improved its model ([SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/llms-txt/)). SISTRIX reported "no measurable effect" across 96,418 pages ([bediscoverable.ai](https://bediscoverable.ai/resources/geo-tactics-ai-citations-study)). Log analyses report AI crawlers almost never request the file ([richdevtools](https://richdevtools.com/articles/web/llms-txt-evidence-2026)), and no major platform documents reading it in production. John Mueller compared it to the keywords meta tag ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-says-llms-txt-comparable-to-keywords-meta-tag/544804/)). No study was found on `.md` page copies either way, for benefit or for harm. The honest case for both files is the agent use case: a coding agent or MCP server pointed at the site. That is a real use, but it is not search.

**What correlates with being cited is ordinary ranking plus concrete answers, and the correlation is weakening.** Ahrefs found 76% of AI Overview citations ranked in the top 10 in 2025, but only **38% in 2026** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/search-rankings-ai-citations); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-ai-overview-citations-from-top-ranking-pages-drop-sharply/568637/)). That fits Google's documented "query fan-out" into subtopics. The one experiment, the Princeton GEO paper, raised visibility by up to about 40% by adding citations, quotations and statistics. It ran on a research engine, not on production systems ([arXiv 2311.09735](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.09735)). The cost of AI answers is measured: Pew found users clicked a result on **8% of visits with an AI summary against 15% without** ([Search Engine Journal on Pew](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/pew-research-confirms-google-ai-overviews-is-eroding-web-ecosystem/551825/)). For a plumber or a trainer, that pushes value toward transactional, local and branded queries. The page has to make the business, place, price and contact route unmistakable, because a mention converts only if the reader acts on it.

Every line in the table is accurate. **Of its roughly eighteen rules, about ten restate an audit check, a GUIDE section or another skill.** The fleet review explains why that matters. On a1k9training "both arms put the town in titles and headings, made the course name the one `<h1>`, moved the structured data off the slogans, and advised on the Google Business Profile, which the skill does not mention". On pro-plumbing, "only the arm with it rewrote the titles into searchers' words". On k9-solutions, "the arm with the skill wrote the tighter description, front-loaded as the skill asks". The review's verdict is that "its one visible effect was titles and descriptions closer to its own build-mode rules … it has not yet earned its place. The next question is what it knows that the model does not." The research answers that question. The model already knows the stable basics, and the fleet arms showed it. What it plausibly does not know, or applies unevenly, falls into three groups. The first is facts dated after or near its training: FAQ removed in June 2026, Course info in 2025, Cloudflare's 2026-09-15 defaults. The second is traps where the intuitive move is wrong: `Disallow` to hide a page, blocking every AI bot to stop "scraping", self-serving review stars, a page per town. The third is claims vendors push that platforms deny: llms.txt and schema as AI-citation levers.

A caution on evidence quality. The fleet measurement ran each arm once on one model, and the review itself calls it "a signal, not a verdict". Its brief ("Hardly anyone finds us on Google; improve that") did not probe AI search, structured-data type choice, area pages or crawler policy. Those are exactly where the research says the skill could add value, so the measurement has not yet tested the lines proposed below.

Two candidates do **not** belong in `kiss-seo`. Author and owner identity (a real named person, no AI headshots, AI disclosure where readers expect it) is a words-and-facts rule. `kiss-writing-for-the-web` already holds the neighbouring "Never invent a fact", so a one-line extension there keeps the rule in one place. Google Business Profile advice (matching name, phone and hours; the real trading name) was given by **both** arms on two sites without the skill. By the design philosophy's own test, adding it would be wording, not outcome. The exception is the narrow trap the model might not volunteer: no keywords in the profile name, because Google can suspend the profile for it. That fits in A2's line. Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools verification at launch is an operator hand-over step, not a site property, and no build can measure it. If it goes anywhere, it goes in `kiss-site-review`'s hand-over.

### Cuts

Cut C3's heading rule. Cut the "Links say where they go" line, which belongs to `kiss-writing-for-the-web`. Cut the `og:image` line, which the audit checks with an exact fix in `kiss-site-review`. Cut the "In a kiss site" bullets on canonical, `trailingSlash`, `aliases` and the config seam, which `kiss-site-new`, `kiss-information-architecture` and `kiss-page-add` carry. In review mode, cut the re-listing of the ten audit ids and the `Disallow: /` paragraph. Replace them with one sentence that defers to `kiss-site-review` step 1 for anything the audit fires on. What stays is one purpose per page, titles and descriptions written for the searcher (the only measured effect), noindex-not-disallow, structured data that matches the page, and A1–A5. That is perhaps half the current length. A cut is safe to make without a new measurement when the line restates an audit check, because the audit fires whether or not the skill is loaded. Every _addition_ should wait for its brief to show a difference, because that is the standard `CLAUDE.md` sets: "judged by the sites they produce, not by code review."

## Conclusion

The research turns the fleet result from an embarrassment into a design rule. A domain skill restating stable, well-known SEO practice will measure as no difference, because the model already carries it. A domain skill's value is concentrated in three places: what changed after training (rich-result removals, crawler splits, CDN defaults), what is counter-intuitive (noindex not disallow, no stars for yourself, no page per town), and what vendors oversell (llms.txt, schema for AI). Those are also the lines a with-and-without brief can be written to provoke. The present brief probed none of them, which is why the skill has neither passed nor properly failed.

The sharper finding is that kiss's own **mechanism** carries the two clearest departures from platform guidance. One is a sitemap whose `lastmod` is the build time on every page. The other is a GUIDE sentence that markets `llms.txt` to answer engines with no evidence that any engine reads it. Both are cheap to fix. Both matter more than any wording change to the skill, because every kiss site inherits them whether or not an agent ever opens `kiss-seo`. Fix the engine first. Then shrink the skill to its traps and measure each trap with a brief built to trigger it.
