# Local SEO best practice in 2026 for small UK service businesses with a static site

Scope: plumber, dog trainer, wedding service, training provider with a venue. Researched 2026-11-01. Each source is labelled as Google documentation, UK law/regulator, or practitioner opinion. Practitioner surveys (Whitespark, BrightLocal) record what experts or consumers say, not ranking measurements.

## 1. Google's documented local ranking factors and current guidance

### Takeaway

Google's own help page (live in 2026) still names three factors: relevance, distance and prominence. It lists verification, complete and accurate profile info (address if customers visit, hours including special hours, category, attributes), reviews and replies, and photos. Prominence explicitly includes "how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have". The page gives no separate tip about the website. Google says ranking cannot be paid for.

### Cited Findings

- Google documentation: "Relevance is how well a Business Profile matches what someone is searching for." "Distance refers to how far each business is from the customer who's searching." "Prominence means how well-known a business is" and draws on info such as "how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have" — [Google Business Profile Help, Tips to improve your local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)
- Google documentation: "Businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local search results." The page lists a full address (only if customers can visit), hours including special hours, category and attributes. "More reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking." "There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google." — [same page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)
- Google documentation: in the guidelines for representing your business, the profile's website field should be "a website that represents your individual business location". Use a local phone number rather than a central helpline where possible. Don't link to pages, including social media pages, that redirect elsewhere. Premium-rate numbers are not allowed — [Guidelines for representing your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)
- Google documentation: the business name must match the real-world name on signage, the website and stationery. "Including unnecessary information in your business name isn't permitted": no taglines, service descriptions or location details. Doing so "could result in the suspension" of the profile. There is "only one profile per business" per location — [same page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)
- Practitioner survey: Whitespark's 2026 Local Search Ranking Factors (published 6 Nov 2025; 47 experts scored 187 factors; the first edition in about two years) gives this top-5 for the local pack/Maps: 1 Primary GBP category, 2 Proximity of address to the point of search, 3 Keywords in GBP business title, 4 Physical address in city of search, 5 Business is open at time of search. Top 3 for local organic: 1 Dedicated page for each service, 2 Geographic (city/neighbourhood) keyword relevance of content, 3 Quality/authority of inbound links to domain — [Whitespark LSRF](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/)
- Practitioner survey: for 2026, Whitespark's text says review signals and behavioural signals rose in importance, while on-page and link signals dropped slightly. Whitespark warns that the experts "are opinions, not insider data" — [Whitespark LSRF](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/); [Whitespark Local Insider newsletter](https://whitespark.beehiiv.com/p/the-2026-local-search-ranking-factors-are-here)
- Unverified: a third-party summary gives these group weights: GBP signals 32%, reviews 20%, on-page 15%. I could not confirm them in Whitespark's text, because the weights are in a chart image — [Whitehat SEO summary](https://whitehat-seo.co.uk/blog/mastering-google-business-profile)

### Inferences

- The things a website build controls map to "relevance" (service pages, location content) and "prominence" (links, mentions). Proximity and the category are outside its control.
- "Keywords in GBP business title" ranks highly in the survey, but Google's name policy forbids adding them. A site build should use the real trading name, the same on the site as on the profile, and never invent a keyword-stuffed name.
- A dedicated page for each service is the factor experts rank first for local organic and second for AI visibility. That supports one page per service over one page listing every service.

### Gaps

- Google publishes no weights. The survey's group percentages could not be checked from text.

## 2. Google Business Profile, 2023–2026: what changed, and how the site and the profile should agree

### Takeaway

Since 2023 the profile has lost the features that let it stand in for a website: the free websites (March 2024), chat and call history (July 2024) and Q&A (late 2025, replaced by Gemini "Ask Maps"). The profile is now a listing that points at the business's own site, and an AI layer reads both. The profile and the site should state the same name, phone, hours and services.

### Cited Findings

- Business Profile websites (business.site / negocio.site): announced January 2024 for shutdown in March 2024, then redirected to the profile until 10 June 2024, after which they returned "page not found". Google's support page says "as of 5 March 2024" they are no longer available. Search Engine Land reported 1 March, so sources disagree on the exact day — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/websites-created-with-google-business-profiles-to-shut-down-in-march/505413/); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-business-profile-websites-are-now-redirected-to-google-maps-438154); [Google Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7178589?hl=en-IE)
- Chat and call history: wind-down began 15 July 2024; fully unsupported on 31 July 2024 — [Google Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/14919062); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-business-profile-chat-and-call-history-going-away-442666)
- Q&A: the API was reportedly discontinued on 3 Nov 2025, with public deprecation from 3 Dec 2025, replaced by "Ask Maps" (Gemini), which answers from the profile (services, attributes, hours, description, categories) and from reviews. Sources are marketing blogs and their dates disagree slightly. One says Ask Maps was US-only and limited to some categories at first — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-quietly-kills-q-a-for-an-ai-button-most-wont-use/); [Footbridge Media](https://www.footbridgemedia.com/marketing-tips/google-shutting-down-gbp-q-a); [The HOTH](https://www.thehoth.com/blog/google-cuts-qa-sections/)
- Google Maps redesign on 12 March 2026: added Ask Maps (conversational, Gemini-powered), drawing on "300 million places and 500 million reviews" — [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/google-maps-gains-gemini-powered-navigation-features-20cb7973); [Localogy](https://www.localogy.com/2026/02/google-extends-conversational-ai-to-maps/embed/)
- Verification: a secondary source says video verification became required for most businesses from January 2026. It also attributes to Sterling Sky the figure that 42% of suspended profiles failed at address verification. Unverified against Sterling Sky directly — [First AI Movers](https://www.firstaimovers.com/p/google-business-profile-video-verification-ai-entity-2026)
- Google documentation: a profile may be suspended for content that breaks the guidelines or misrepresents the business — [Guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)

### Inferences

- For the site build: the site is now the only rich, owned surface. Business facts (services, prices, area served, hours, FAQs) belong on the site, written so that both people and AI can quote them. FAQ content that used to live in GBP Q&A should move to the site.
- Agreement checklist: the same trading name, the same phone number (a local number, not premium-rate), the same hours, and the profile's website field pointing at the site's home page (or a location page for a multi-location business). The profile's services and categories should match the site's service pages.

### Gaps

- I did not find Sterling Sky's own dated timeline or posts on suspensions of service-area businesses (the search returned nothing relevant). The suspension-rate claims are second-hand.
- Google has published no dated changelog for Business Profile features. Dates come from press reports.

## 3. NAP consistency and citations in 2026

### Takeaway

NAP consistency still matters, but less, and mostly as a hygiene and trust factor. Experts think citations' share of map-pack ranking is falling, but they have new weight as mentions that AI search reads. Google's own help page does not mention NAP or citations at all, only links and reviews.

### Cited Findings

- Practitioner survey: Darren Shaw: "I was surprised to see that citations lost about half a percentage point", continuing a downward trend. "In AI SEO, mentions (citations) are the new link." "3 of the top 5 AI Search visibility factors are citation factors." — [Whitespark LSRF 2026](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/)
- Practitioner survey: "HTML NAP matching GBP NAP" ranks 15th for the local pack (score 153), 55th for local organic and 68th for AI search. "Consistency of citations on primary search engines" ranks 28th for the local pack, 71st for local organic and 31st for AI. The top AI-visibility factor is "Presence of business on expert-curated 'best of' and similar lists" (179), and third is "Prominence on key industry-relevant domains" — [Whitespark LSRF 2026](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/)
- Practitioner opinion: AI Mode is said to draw on business websites, press mentions, social media and directories such as Yelp, with Maps content still central (undated) — [SOCi](https://www.soci.ai/?p=33265)

### Inferences

- For a site build: print the NAP as crawlable HTML text (not an image), matching the profile exactly, in the footer or on the contact page. That is the 15th-ranked map-pack factor, and the site has full control over it.
- Bulk directory submission is low value. A few accurate core listings plus mentions on relevant UK trade and industry sites (trade bodies, wedding directories, training accreditation bodies) and "best of" lists are worth more, especially for AI visibility.

### Gaps

- BrightLocal's latest citation or local ranking survey was not retrieved.

## 4. Service-area businesses: hiding the address, area pages and doorway risk

### Takeaway

Google is explicit: a business that doesn't serve customers at its address (a home-based plumber or mobile dog trainer) must remove the address from its profile. It can set up to 20 named service areas (no radius) within about two hours' drive. City pages are allowed, but Google's spam policy names "pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page" as doorway abuse. Google gives no numeric limit.

### Cited Findings

- Google documentation: "If you don't serve customers at your business address, remove your address from your Business Profile." "You can set up to 20 service areas based on the cities, postal codes, or other areas you serve." "You can't set your service area as a radius distance around your business." "The boundaries of your overall area shouldn't be more than about 2 hours of driving time from where your business is based." — [Google Help, manage your service area](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481)
- Google documentation: a business that runs from a residential address and serves customers elsewhere should hide that address (the example given is a plumber working from home). A hybrid business (a staffed premises that also travels) can show the address and set a service area. Virtual offices and mailing-only addresses are not eligible unless staffed during business hours — [Guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)
- Google documentation (spam policies): "Doorway abuse is when sites or pages are created to rank for specific, similar search queries." Examples: "Having multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page"; "Creating substantially similar pages that are closer to search results than a clearly defined, browseable hierarchy." — [Google Search spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)
- Practitioner survey: "Physical address in city of search" is the 4th-ranked map-pack factor, and "Geographic keyword relevance of content" is 2nd for local organic — [Whitespark LSRF 2026](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/)

### Inferences

- A service-area business with a hidden address ranks in the map pack mainly near its verified (hidden) location. Area pages on the site serve local organic results, not the map pack.
- A rule a site build can apply: an area page is justified only when it has content specific to that place (jobs done there, local testimonials, travel or parking notes, local regulations, a venue's directions). If the only difference between pages is the town name, they are doorways. Keep the area pages within the GBP service area (at most 20 areas, about 2 hours' drive), so the site and the profile agree.
- A venue-based training provider is a storefront: show the address, embed a map, give directions and parking. A wedding service that travels is usually a service-area or hybrid business.

### Gaps

- Google gives no number of town pages that counts as too many. Any figure is practitioner folklore, and I found no reliable source for one.
- Sterling Sky research on service-area business suspensions or hidden-address ranking was not retrieved.

## 5. Reviews: testimonials, schema, the review link, and UK fake-review law

### Takeaway

Reviews are documented by Google as a ranking input and rated by experts as rising in importance. A business's own site cannot get review stars for itself: Google treats LocalBusiness/Organization reviews the business controls as "self-serving" and ineligible. In the UK, since 6 April 2025, fake reviews and concealed incentivised reviews are banned under the DMCC Act 2024. The CMA can fine up to 10% of global turnover, and anyone publishing reviews must take reasonable steps and have a policy.

### Cited Findings

- Google documentation: if "the entity that's being reviewed controls the reviews about itself", pages using "LocalBusiness or any other type of Organization structured data" "are ineligible for star review feature". This includes a business's own site showing its reviews in markup or through an embedded Google or Facebook reviews widget. LocalBusiness review snippets are only for sites that review other businesses — [Google Search Central, review snippet](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet)
- Google documentation: "More reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking"; "Positive reviews and helpful replies can help your business stand out." — [GBP Help 7091](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)
- UK law: the DMCC Act prohibitions on fake reviews, concealed incentivised reviews, and false or misleading consumer review information took effect on 6 April 2025. The CMA published guidance (CMA208) with a three-month grace period. Publishing reviews misleadingly, such as cherry-picking positive reviews or using outdated feedback, is covered. Anyone who publishes or provides access to reviews must take "reasonable and proportionate steps". The guidance expects a clear policy prohibiting fake reviews that sets out the trader's approach to incentivised reviews. Penalties can reach 10% of global turnover — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/no-more-faux-five-stars-the-dmcc-act-bans-fake-reviews); [RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/consumer/spring-2025/cma-guidance-on-unfair-commercial-practices-under-the-dmcca/); [techUK](https://www.techuk.org/insights/news/fake-reviews-and-the-dmcc-act-what-the-cma-s-actions-mean-for-uk-businesses)
- Enforcement: the CMA reviewed more than 100 businesses' websites and found over half could be failing to comply with the guidance. Enforcement began after the grace period (July 2025) — [Freshfields](https://riskandcompliance.freshfields.com/post/102kxvj/cma-signals-end-of-grace-period-fake-reviews-enforcement-steps-up); [techUK](https://www.techuk.org/insights/news/fake-reviews-and-the-dmcc-act-what-the-cma-s-actions-mean-for-uk-businesses)
- Google's UK undertakings to the CMA (24 Jan 2025): warning alerts on the profiles of businesses found using fake reviews, possible deactivation of a business's reviews, global bans for UK fake reviewers, and three years of reporting to the CMA. These cover business reviews on Search and Maps — [GOV.UK press release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-secures-important-changes-from-google-to-tackle-fake-reviews)
- Consumer survey: BrightLocal's Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 (published 11 Feb 2026). Secondary reports say 97% of consumers read reviews of local businesses and 41% always do (29% the year before). Google's share of review use fell from 83% to 71%, while Apple Maps rose from 14% to 27%. Use of AI tools for local discovery rose from 6% to 45%. Figures come from secondary write-ups, one of which gives a conflicting Google share (81%) — [BrightLocal LCRS](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/); [PinMeTo summary](https://www.pinmeto.com/news/brightlocal-local-consumer-review-survey-2026/); [shno.co](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/local-seo-statistics)

### Inferences

- For a site build: on-site testimonials are fine as content, but there should be no AggregateRating/Review markup about the business itself (it is ineligible, and claiming it risks a manual action). Show reviews honestly: attribute them, date them, and don't present only the best ones in a misleading way (CMA). Add a short review policy page, which the CMA guidance expects of anyone publishing reviews. Link to the GBP "write a review" URL to ask for reviews, and ask every customer, not only the happy ones.
- The rise of Apple Maps in the consumer survey suggests also claiming Apple Business Connect, though that is a survey of consumers, not of ranking.

### Gaps

- Google's prohibited-content policy text on incentivised reviews and review gating was not fetched in this session.
- The primary BrightLocal 2026 figures were not read directly, because secondary sources conflict.
- The current CMA208 page on GOV.UK was not opened directly. Its contents come from law-firm summaries.

## 6. LocalBusiness structured data: does it help local ranking?

### Takeaway

Google's documentation describes LocalBusiness markup as feeding knowledge-panel and carousel display and makes no ranking claim. I found no primary Google statement that it boosts local ranking, and practitioner consensus is that its benefit is indirect. Mark it up cheaply and accurately, but don't expect it to move rankings.

### Cited Findings

- Google documentation: Search "may display a prominent Google knowledge panel with details about a business". Required properties are `address` and `name`. Recommended: `geo`, `openingHoursSpecification`, `telephone`, `url`, `priceRange`, `department`, and others. `aggregateRating` and `review` are "only recommended for sites that capture reviews about other local businesses." "Google does not guarantee that features that consume structured data will show up." The page makes no statement about ranking — [Google Search Central, local business](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business)
- Practitioner opinion: schema is not a direct ranking factor but helps search engines understand the content and can lift click-through through rich results. The attributions to Mueller are second-hand — [Safari Digital](https://www.safaridigital.com.au/?p=11066); [Bird Marketing](https://bird.marketing/blog/digital-marketing/guide/local-seo/structured-data-local-seo/)

### Inferences

- Emit one LocalBusiness (most specific subtype, e.g. `Plumber`) JSON-LD block from the same data that renders the visible NAP and hours, so the markup and the page cannot disagree. A service-area business whose address is hidden in GBP should still give at least a locality or region and `areaServed`. A full street address is required for the rich feature, which means a trade-off for home-based businesses.

### Gaps

- I found no verbatim, dated Google statement (Mueller or Sullivan) on LocalBusiness markup and local pack ranking.

## 7. Click-to-call, hours, map embeds, contact pages, and AI Overviews / AI Mode

### Takeaway

Google documents being open, hours and phone numbers as profile-quality factors, and experts rate "open at time of search" 5th for the map pack. AI surfaces (AI Overviews, AI Mode, Gemini in Maps, Ask Maps) now answer local queries from the profile, reviews and the business's own site. The evidence on traffic impact is early-stage and anecdotal.

### Cited Findings

- Practitioner survey: "Business is open at time of search" is the 5th map-pack factor — [Whitespark LSRF 2026](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/)
- Google documentation: provide "regular customer-facing hours" and special hours. Use a local number under the business's direct control — [GBP guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177); [GBP Help 7091](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)
- Gemini began showing local results as visual cards with Maps photos and ratings in December 2025. Google has not said whether businesses need to do anything to appear — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/gemini-now-serves-local-results-from-google-maps-in-visual-format/)
- Google tested replacing the local map pack with AI Overviews (report from June 2025). I found no evidence that this became the default — [DAC Group](https://www.dacgroup.com/insights/local-search-news/google-tests-replacing-local-map-pack-with-ai-overviews/)
- Practitioner survey: the 2026 Whitespark survey added AI-search-visibility scoring with 47 new factors. The top factors are presence on curated "best of" lists, a dedicated page for each service, and prominence on industry-relevant domains — [Whitespark LSRF 2026](https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/)
- Consumer survey: AI tools are used for local discovery by 45%, up from 6% (BrightLocal 2026, via a secondary source) — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/online-review-statistics-2026-consumer-trust-data)

### Inferences

- For a site build: put a `tel:` link with the same number as the profile in the header on mobile. Show hours as text, with special and holiday hours. A storefront or venue gets a contact page with the address, directions, parking and a lazy-loaded map embed. A service-area business shows the list of areas served instead of a map pin at home. Each service gets its own page with clear prices, process and FAQs, which AI answers can quote. Provide an `llms.txt` and Markdown copies where the engine supports them.
- A map embed has no documented ranking effect. It serves the visitor, so it should be lazy-loaded and should not block rendering.

### Gaps

- I found no independent measurement of how much local-pack click share has moved to AI Overviews, AI Mode or Ask Maps for UK service queries. Whether AI Mode or Ask Maps has rolled out in the UK, and when, was not confirmed.
- I found no Google documentation on click-to-call or map embeds as ranking inputs.
