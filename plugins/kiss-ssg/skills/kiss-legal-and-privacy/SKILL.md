# Legal and privacy

A site's legal pages are claims about what the business does with people's details. They are only as true as the inventory behind them and the owner's habits after launch. This skill is method; the law is in one reference per jurisdiction, each fact dated and sourced.

**This is not legal advice.** It finds what a small site usually gets wrong and drafts what the law lists. Consumer contracts (deposits, cancellation, refunds), terms and conditions, liability, and anything the owner is worried about go to an adviser: flag them, never draft them.

## The method

1. **Ask the jurisdiction first**, and where the owner is based, unless the site's `AIKB/` or its pages already say. Open the matching reference: [references/uk.md](references/uk.md) or [references/eu.md](references/eu.md). For anywhere else there is no reference yet: say so, flag it, and advise a local adviser.

2. **Take an inventory before drafting anything**: every place personal data enters or leaves the site.
   - **From the build.** `npx kiss-ssg check <site-script>` prints the report as JSON on stdout (the build's own log goes to stderr); `--summary` prints the readable lines instead. Its `thirdParty` key lists each page's third-party origins and what loads them (`script`, `iframe`, `img`, `form-action`, `stylesheet`, `preload`, `preconnect`). Its audit has three checks of this skill's: `privacy-link-missing` (a form that asks for an email or phone number and does not link the privacy page), `checkbox-prechecked` (a ticked box whose label offers marketing) and `placeholder-left` (a `<mark>` or `[to confirm` shipping). Eight more are the consent manager's, and this skill's too: `consent-missing` (a tracker or embed with no `config.consent`), `consent-ungated` (a third party nothing holds back), `consent-ads-cmp` (AdSense or Ad Manager), `consent-objection-third-party`, `consent-settings-missing`, `consent-google-advanced`, `consent-records-missing` and `consent-history-missing` (the page `ui.history` names has no `{{consentRecord}}`). On a site with `config.consent`, `report.consent` lists every declared service with its category and how it is gated, and the banner hash each page carries; in `thirdParty`, an origin carrying `services` loads only after consent. Then read the templates for forms (each field, and the backend: `data-netlify`, an `action` URL), `mailto:` and `tel:` links, and analytics. The host is a processor too: it keeps logs.
   - **A font from a third-party CDN (Google Fonts) is a page fix, not a question:** self-host it, with its licence file beside it, and drop the CDN `<link>` and `preconnect`. **Whether an analytics or tracking tag stays, switches or goes is the owner's decision:** never remove one on your own; report what it sends and before what, and ask.
   - **An empty `thirdParty` proves nothing.** It reads static markup only. An iframe a script injects (a facade a site wrote itself, a chat widget) and a stylesheet's CSS `@import` are invisible to it; `{{embed}}`'s frames are listed. Read the scripts and the CSS as well.
   - **Asked of the owner:** the email provider; who else sees enquiries, **including the site's own developer and administrator**; payments; messaging groups; paper records; photos of people; any intent to send marketing; and **whether any field asks about health or another special category** (the reference lists them): if one does, it goes to an adviser before the notice is written.

3. **Measure storage in a real browser, never from the templates.** Look at cookies, `localStorage` and IndexedDB on every page template, signed out, at a phone width and a desktop width (a script can load an embed at one width only): first after **at least 10 seconds idle with no interaction** (a timer can load an embed with no click: one measured site did at 6 seconds), then after scrolling to the bottom, then after one click. Read the storage of every origin, the embed's frames included. How to get the site in front of a browser is the [browser pass](../kiss-site-review/browser-pass.md); without a browser, say the storage check did not run. A `youtube-nocookie.com` embed was measured writing `yt-icons-last-purged` and `ytidb::LAST_RESULT_ENTRY_KEY` to `localStorage` as soon as it loaded (2026-11-02), on a page that said no cookie banner was needed. A "privacy-enhanced" embed is not storage-free. The fix is `{{embed}}`, which holds the iframe back until the visitor loads it, on any site: with no `config.consent` it is a click-to-load facade, and under `config.consent` the visitor's stored choice governs it too ([`kiss-front-end`](../kiss-front-end/SKILL.md)). Measure again after the fix, and after a refusal: nothing gated may store anything.

4. **Write the notice from the law's checklist, checked against the inventory.** The reference lists what a notice must contain. Every item gets a sentence, or a reason it does not apply. **Delete every statement the inventory does not support.** Adapting another organisation's policy is the normal case, and its claims are the hazard: analytics the site does not run, location data, accounts, a consent centre. Say only what actually happens, including in the owner's tools (archiving a WhatsApp group does not remove its members).

5. **Mark every fact the owner has not given** as `<mark>[to confirm: …]</mark>`, in the page, visible. Nothing ships while one remains: the audit's `placeholder-left` reports it. Never write a legal fact the owner did not give, however likely it looks ("nobody else sees enquiries" was false: the site's administrator could).

6. **Keep the form's line as true as the page.** The line beside a form says who receives the details, what they are used for, and that the visitor can ask for them to be deleted, and links the privacy page. When the page changes, the line changes in the same edit: "only to reply to you" became false the day the owner reserved the right to send offers. A marketing opt-out on the form is an **unticked** box.

7. **Sort every finding into one of four lanes:**
   - **page wording:** you fix it;
   - **owner habit:** it goes in the handout (step 9);
   - **owner decision:** the owner accepts the risk, recorded (step 8);
   - **adviser:** flagged, never drafted.

8. **Record each accepted risk** in the format the [review protocol](../kiss-site-review/review-protocol.md) gives, so the next review reads it as a decision, not an oversight. Where the risk is a legal duty left unmet, the record names the duty plainly; whether a workaround meets it is an adviser's question, not yours. The owner makes the decision; when someone else made it for them, say so and ask the owner to confirm it. In review, a record out of that format, with no duty named, or that presents a workaround as the fix is itself a finding.

9. **Produce the owner handout**, one page, from [references/owner-handout.md](references/owner-handout.md). The site's promises depend on habits nobody can see from the build.

10. **Verify a vendor's claim at its source** (a data processing agreement, a transfer certification), and record the URL and the date beside the claim. **Never link a deep URL you have not loaded.** A regulator's site may refuse an automated fetch (ico.org.uk returned 403 on 2026-11-02 in one session and loaded in another); where it does, link the home page or say what to search for.

## Build mode

`kiss-site-new` and `kiss-page-add` call this when a site gets a form, an embed, analytics or any third-party script. A site with a form ships with, from day one:

- a privacy page, written by steps 2–6 with every unconfirmed fact marked, at the id `privacy` (or named in `config.audit.privacyPage`), linked from the footer (`kiss-information-architecture`) and from every form that asks for an email or phone number;
- the line beside each form (step 6), with `{{link "privacy"}}`;
- the owner handout and the list of `[to confirm` marks, at the hand-over.

A site with analytics, an advertising tag or any other third-party script declares each one in `config.consent`, with its embeds, and loads them only through `{{consentScript}}` and `{{embed}}`, so nothing it loads stores anything before the visitor allows it. A library the site cannot work without, from a CDN, is a `necessary` service naming its `origins`. The layout has `{{consentHead}}` after the meta tags, `{{consentBanner}}` first in `<body>` and `{{consentSettings}}` in the footer, and the cookie page has `{{consentTable}}`. The privacy page has `{{consentRecord}}`, the visitor's own history of choices, and `ui.history` names that page, so the settings dialog links it (GUIDE.md § Cookie consent; `examples/14-consent`). A site whose only third parties are videos, maps or posts, and that asks about nothing else, needs no banner: `{{embed}}` with no `config.consent` loads nothing until the visitor presses its button, and stores no choice. The privacy page still names each provider and what loading it sends them. The decisions in it are this skill's:

- **Declare what each service stores**, from the browser measurement (step 3): its first-party cookies and `storage` keys. Withdrawal deletes only what is declared. Another site's cookies and an embed's frame storage cannot be deleted from the page, and the cookie page says so.
- **The category is a legal claim.** `statistics` with `mode: 'objection'` is the UK's statistics exception (the reference: [references/uk.md](references/uk.md)) and nothing wider. Never put a service there on its name alone, never on an EU site, and never a third party's analytics. `gtm` stays `marketing`, its default, unless the owner confirms every tag in the container that is not Google's has its own consent check.
- **Records.** A site with a Google advertising tag sets `endpoint` to a store on the owner's own server, because Google's EU user consent policy asks for each consent's date and time and the text shown (`consent-records-missing`). The text shown is the banner hash in the build's knowledge base, and it dates a wording only if `npx kiss-ssg aikb <site-script>` is run and committed **before** a banner change is deployed. Put that in the handout. The notice says what the server keeps of each record, and for how long: the history page lists what is sent and nothing more, so the notice is where that sentence lives. The history in the visitor's browser is their copy, not the evidence.
- **AdSense, Ad Manager or AdMob need a Google-certified CMP**, and kiss's is not one (`consent-ads-cmp`). That is an owner decision, recorded: a certified CMP, or no publisher ads. GA4 and Google Ads conversion tracking need none.
- **How long a choice lasts.** The choice cookie keeps for 182 days by default, the six months the ICO suggests before asking again (the reference). A new service or a new third party asks everyone again on its own; wording and colours do not.
- **`google.mode: 'advanced'`** sends Google cookieless pings before any choice, which no regulator has said is lawful (`consent-google-advanced`). It is an owner decision, recorded, never a default you choose.

## Review mode

Report to the [review protocol](../kiss-site-review/review-protocol.md): every finding before anything is edited, with its priority, its measurement and its lane, one row per cause. A recorded accepted risk is not a finding. Read the audit and `thirdParty` first, then the browser (step 3), then the notice against the reference's checklist item by item: report each item as present (quote it), missing, untrue against the inventory, or not applicable. Then the who-we-are details, any reviews or ratings, and the rights in every image, font and text the site did not make (the reference says what each needs).

These are **P1** (a legal duty): a notice missing a checklist item, storage before consent, a consent banner where refusing is harder than accepting, a pre-ticked marketing box, no geographic address or email address for the business, a form with no line, a special-category question with no adviser's answer, a price that leaves out a compulsory fee, no written processor contract with someone who can see enquiries (often the site's own developer or administrator), and a `[to confirm` mark on a page about to ship. Check too that the notice names the regulator as it is now called (the reference says), and that its "is giving details required" answer matches the fields the form actually requires.
