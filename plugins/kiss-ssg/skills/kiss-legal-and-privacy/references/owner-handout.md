# The owner handout: a template

One page for the owner, in plain words, handed over with the site. The site's privacy page makes promises; this page is the habits that keep them true. Fill each line from the inventory and the owner's answers, delete the lines that do not apply, and keep every fact the owner has not confirmed marked `[to confirm: …]`. Write it in the owner's voice ("you"), not the law's. Cite no deep URL you have not loaded; a regulator's home page and a search term are enough.

It is not legal advice, and says so in its last line.

---

**Your website's privacy promises: what you do to keep them**

_[Business name], [date]. Your site's privacy page is at [URL]._

**Once a year**

- [ ] **The data protection fee.** Check whether you have to pay it, and pay it if so. Search "data protection fee self assessment" on ico.org.uk; it takes about 10 minutes. [UK only]
- [ ] **Read the privacy page again.** If anything you do has changed (a new email provider, a newsletter, a booking system, a new person helping), the page changes too. Ask whoever looks after the site.

**Every time someone gets in touch**

- [ ] **Keep only what you need, and delete it when the time is up.** The privacy page says you keep enquiries for [period]. Put a reminder in your calendar to clear old enquiries from your inbox, phone and paper notes.
- [ ] **Someone says "no offers, thanks".** Write their address on your do-not-send list straight away, and check the list before every offer you send. [Only if the site says you may send offers.]
- [ ] **Someone asks what you hold about them, or asks you to delete it.** Reply without delay: the law sets a time limit. If you are unsure, search "right of access" on ico.org.uk or ask for advice before you reply. A complaint about how you handled their details gets a reply saying you have it within 30 days. [UK]

**Photos and messages**

- [ ] **Photos of people.** Only use a photo of a client (or their child, or their dog with them in it) on the site or social media when they have said yes, and keep a note of that yes: who, when, and for what.
- [ ] **Group chats.** A WhatsApp or similar group shows every member's phone number to every other member. Archiving the group hides it on your phone; it does not remove anyone. Remove people who have left, or use a broadcast list instead.

**Your accounts**

- [ ] **Two-step sign-in** on your email, the website host and the form service.
- [ ] **A written agreement with everyone who can see enquiries for you**: the email provider, the website host, the form service, and **whoever builds or looks after your website**. For the big providers it is usually their standard data processing terms, which you accept in your account settings; for a person, it is a short written agreement. [Name them.]

**If something goes wrong**

A lost phone or laptop with enquiries on it, an email sent to the wrong person, an account someone else got into:

1. Write down what happened, when you found out, whose details were involved and what you did about it. Keep this note for every slip, even one you do not report.
2. Change passwords and lock the account or device if you can.
3. Report it to the regulator **within 72 hours** of finding out, **unless it is unlikely to put anyone at risk**. If you are not sure, report it. Search "report a breach" on ico.org.uk, or call their helpline (the number is on their "Contact us" page). [UK; in the EU, your country's data protection authority.]
4. If it is likely to cause serious harm, tell the people involved too.

**Who to ask**

- About the website: [developer or administrator, contact].
- About the law: [adviser, or "find a solicitor"]. This page is a checklist of habits, not legal advice.
