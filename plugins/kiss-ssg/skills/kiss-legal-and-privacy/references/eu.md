# European Union

The law a small site in an EU member state meets, as of 2 November 2026, marked where it differs from [the UK reference](uk.md). Read that file's method notes too: most of the shape is shared, because UK law began as these instruments. Not legal advice.

**A limit of this file:** EUR-Lex (eur-lex.europa.eu) and curia.europa.eu returned no text to an automated fetch on 2026-11-02. Where an EU instrument as adopted is on legislation.gov.uk, it is cited there (the text as adopted, or as it stood on 31 December 2020); a fact checked only through a search excerpt says so. ## The privacy notice (GDPR Art. 13)

The same checklist as the UK's, with two differences (checked 2026-11-02 against the text as adopted, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/13/adopted):

- **No complaint to the controller.** The EU text has no 13(2)(ca); the complaint right is to a **supervisory authority** (13(2)(d)), meaning the member state's data protection authority, named.
- **Transfers** refer to an adequacy decision by the European Commission and the safeguards of Arts 46, 47 or 49(1) (13(1)(f)), not to UK adequacy regulations.

The UK's "recognised legitimate interest" (Art. 6(1)(ea)) does not exist in the EU text: it was inserted by the UK's Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/6). The other articles the UK reference cites (7, 14, 28, 30, 32, 33 and 34) carry the same numbers in the EU text; this file did not compare their wording, so read them on EUR-Lex before quoting one. A breach goes to the supervisory authority, not the UK's Commission.

**Differs from the UK:** the data protection fee is a UK regulation (the Data Protection (Charges and Information) Regulations 2018). Whether a member state has a registration or fee rule of its own is the national authority's to say: not checked here.

## Cookies and storage (ePrivacy Directive art. 5(3))

Storing or reading information on a visitor's device needs their consent after clear and comprehensive information, except storage solely to carry a communication, or strictly necessary for a service the visitor explicitly requested (art. 5(3) as substituted by Directive 2009/136/EC, checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2002/58/article/5).

**Differs from the UK:** there is no exception for statistics or for appearance and functionality in the Directive's text. Those are the UK's Schedule A1 (from 5 February 2026). Analytics storage in the EU needs consent unless the national law or regulator says otherwise.

**It is a directive, so the rule a site meets is the member state's law that implements it,** and national regulators read it differently (on analytics, on consent banners, on "legitimate interest" for storage). Name the member state, read its implementing law and its regulator's guidance, and say in the report which one you read. Never write one rule for "the EU". The EDPB's Guidelines 2/2023 on the technical scope of art. 5(3) (final version dated 16 October 2024 on the page, loaded 2026-11-02, https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-22023-on-technical-scope-of-art-53-of-eprivacy-directive_en) are the shared reading of what counts as storage or access; their contents were not read for this file.

Marketing email is also under the ePrivacy Directive and national law; not checked here. Flag it, and read the national rule.

## Who we are (E-Commerce Directive art. 5)

The UK's Electronic Commerce (EC Directive) Regulations 2002 were made under this Directive, and their reg. 6 lists name, geographic address, contact details including an email address, register, authorisation, regulated profession, VAT number, and clear prices; expect art. 5 to have the same shape. **Unverified at source** on 2026-11-02: legislation.gov.uk's copy of Directive 2000/31/EC did not return the article's text to an automated fetch. Read art. 5 on EUR-Lex. Case C-298/07 applies here directly (see the UK reference, also unverified at source).

Company disclosures follow the member state's company law: not checked here.

## Consumer contracts (flag only)

The Consumer Rights Directive (2011/83/EU) sets the EU's information duties and withdrawal right for distance contracts, implemented nationally. Not checked here. **Flag it for an adviser; never draft the terms.**

## Reviews (the Omnibus Directive)

Directive (EU) 2019/2161 amended the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive on consumer reviews: per commentary found by search on 2026-11-02, it bans claiming reviews come from real customers without reasonable steps to check, and submitting, commissioning or misrepresenting reviews, and requires a trader that publishes reviews to say whether and how it checks them. **Unverified at source:** the text was not loaded. Read the Directive's Annex I points 23b and 23c and art. 7(6) of the amended UCPD on EUR-Lex, and the member state's implementing law, before relying on it. The UK equivalent is the DMCC Act 2024 (the UK reference).

## Copyright

Copyright in the EU is national law over harmonising directives; the method is the UK's: every work the site did not make needs a licence, named, with the attribution the licence asks for. Not checked at source here.

## The accessibility statement (European Accessibility Act)

Directive (EU) 2019/882 applies from 28 June 2025, and exempts microenterprises that provide services (fewer than 10 people, and turnover or balance sheet up to EUR 2 million) (checked 2026-11-02 through search excerpts of https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/882/oj/eng and https://eur-lex.europa.eu/EN/legal-content/summary/accessibility-of-products-and-services.html; the pages themselves were not loaded). Whether a site's service is in its scope is an adviser's question. What the site must do to be accessible is [`kiss-accessibility`](../../kiss-accessibility/SKILL.md)'s; this file adds nothing to it.
