# United Kingdom

The law a small UK site meets, as of 2 November 2026. Every fact carries the date it was checked and the primary source it was checked at. Re-check a fact before relying on it after that date: the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 has been commencing in stages through 2025 and 2026. Not legal advice.

On 30 September 2026 the Information Commission took over the Information Commissioner's functions (checked 2026-11-02, https://www.gov.uk/government/news/information-commission-succeeds-the-ico-as-uks-data-protection-regulator). The legislation now says "the Commission". The regulator's website is still ico.org.uk.

## Data (Use and Access) Act 2025: what is in force

Checked 2026-11-02 against the Act's contents (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/contents) and the Commencement No. 6 Regulations, SI 2026/82, made 29 January 2026 (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/82/made):

- **s.112**, the new cookie and storage rules (PECR reg. 6 and Schedule A1): in force from 5 February 2026.
- **s.114**, the charity soft opt-in (PECR reg. 22(3A)): in force from 5 February 2026.
- **s.115**, the regulator's PECR enforcement powers: in force from 5 February 2026.
- **s.103**, complaints to the controller (DPA 2018 s.164A, UK GDPR Art. 13(2)(ca)): in force from 19 June 2026.
- **Recognised legitimate interests** (UK GDPR Art. 6(1)(ea)): in force from 5 February 2026 (checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/6).

## The privacy notice (UK GDPR Art. 13)

When details are collected from the person, the notice gives (checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/13):

- [ ] the controller's identity and contact details (13(1)(a)), and a data protection officer's if there is one (13(1)(b));
- [ ] each purpose, with its lawful basis (13(1)(c));
- [ ] where the basis is legitimate interests, the interests, named (13(1)(d));
- [ ] the recipients or categories of recipient (13(1)(e)): the email provider, the host, the form backend, the site's developer or administrator;
- [ ] any transfer outside the UK, whether UK adequacy regulations cover it, and the safeguards and how to get a copy where they are relied on (13(1)(f), amended 5 February 2026);
- [ ] how long the details are kept, or how that is decided (13(2)(a));
- [ ] the rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection and portability (13(2)(b));
- [ ] where the basis is consent, the right to withdraw it at any time (13(2)(c));
- [ ] the right to complain to the controller (13(2)(ca), from 19 June 2026);
- [ ] the right to complain to the Commission (13(2)(d));
- [ ] whether giving the details is required, and what happens if not (13(2)(e)), the item the a1k9training draft missed;
- [ ] any automated decision-making, with its safeguards (13(2)(f)).

Details obtained from someone else (a referral, a booking made for another person) need the Art. 14 notice, given within a reasonable period and at the latest within one month (checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/14).

**Special category data** (Art. 9): processing data about health, racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, trade union membership, genetics, biometrics used to identify someone, or sex life or sexual orientation is prohibited unless a condition in Art. 9(2) applies, for example explicit consent (9(2)(a)) (checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/9). A form that asks about any of these (a medical history, an injury, a disability, dietary needs on religious grounds) needs a condition chosen and named in the notice, and may need a data protection impact assessment. **Route it to an adviser**, and report the form as P1 until one has answered.

**Lawful bases** (Art. 6(1)): consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public task, recognised legitimate interest (Annex 1 conditions only), legitimate interests (checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/6). Withdrawing consent must be as easy as giving it (Art. 7(3), checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/7).

**Answering a rights request:** from 5 February 2026 the time limit is in Art. 12A, which Art. 12(3) now points to (checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/12). Art. 12A's own page did not load on 2026-11-02, so its periods are **unverified** here: read them before stating one.

**Complaints to the controller** (DPA 2018 s.164A): the controller must make complaining easy, for example with an electronic complaint form, acknowledge a complaint within 30 days, respond without undue delay and tell the person the outcome (checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2018/12/section/164A). The notice says how to complain.

## The owner's other duties

- **A written contract with every processor** (Art. 28(3), "in writing, including in electronic form", 28(9)): the host, the form backend, the email provider and **the site's own developer or administrator** if they can see enquiries. Art. 28(3)(a)–(h) lists what it must cover (checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/28). A vendor's standard data processing agreement usually is that contract; verify at its source that it applies to the owner's plan.
- **Records of processing** (Art. 30): an organisation under 250 people is exempt unless the processing is likely to be a risk, is not occasional, or includes special-category or criminal-offence data (Art. 30(5), checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/30). Regular enquiries are arguably not occasional: an adviser's question, not yours.
- **Security** appropriate to the risk (Art. 32(1), checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/32): in a small business, mostly two-step sign-in on the email and the host, and deleting what is no longer needed.
- **A breach** is reported to the Commission without undue delay and where feasible within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, unless it is unlikely to result in a risk to people (Art. 33(1), checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/33); every breach is documented, reported or not, with its facts, effects and the remedial action (Art. 33(5), checked 2026-11-02); the people affected are told without undue delay when the risk is high (Art. 34(1), checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/34). The regulator's page is "Report a breach" on ico.org.uk (loaded 2026-11-02, https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/report-a-breach/).
- **The data protection fee** (Data Protection (Charges and Information) Regulations 2018): a controller pays within the first 21 days of each charge period unless all its processing is exempt (reg. 2, checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/480/regulation/2). Tier 1 (turnover up to £632,000 or 10 staff or fewer, or a charity) is £52, tier 2 £78, tier 3 £3,763, £5 less by direct debit (reg. 3, amounts from 17 February 2025, checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/480/regulation/3). The exemptions are in the Schedule (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/480/schedule); whether one applies is the owner's to settle with the regulator's self-assessment (loaded 2026-11-02, https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/self-assessment/).

## Cookies and storage (PECR reg. 6 and Schedule A1)

From 5 February 2026, nobody may store information on, or read it from, a visitor's device except under Schedule A1 (reg. 6(1), checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/6). It covers `localStorage` and IndexedDB as much as cookies, and "instigating" the storage counts (reg. 6(2)(a)): an embed the site loads is the site's storage. Schedule A1 allows (checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/schedule/A1):

- storage **strictly necessary** for a service the visitor asked for (para. 4), or solely to carry a communication (para. 3);
- **statistics** on how the site is used, solely to improve it, with clear and comprehensive information and a simple, free way to object, which the visitor has not used (para. 5);
- adapting the site's **appearance or functionality** to the visitor's preferences, on the same conditions (para. 6);
- locating someone who has asked for **emergency assistance** (para. 7);
- **anything else with consent**, after clear and comprehensive information (para. 2).

No exception covers advertising or tracking by a third party for its own purposes. So a YouTube or Maps embed that writes storage on load needs consent first, or a facade so nothing loads until the visitor clicks.

**The statistics exception is narrow.** Para. 5 needs the storage to be solely for statistics on how the site is used, to improve it, and what it collects must **not be shared** with anyone except to help them improve the site (para. 5(1)(c)), on top of the information and a free way to object (checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/schedule/A1). A third-party analytics product that uses the data for its own purposes does not fit: it needs consent. Whether a given product's terms keep the data to improving this site is a question for the vendor's terms, verified at source, and an adviser when unsure. Never put a tag under para. 5 on its name alone.

**Consent, where it is needed, is the UK GDPR's** (PECR reg. 2 takes its meaning from there, checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/2): "freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous", by "a statement or by a clear affirmative action" (Art. 4(11), checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/4), and as easy to withdraw as to give (Art. 7(3)). So nothing loads before the choice, no box is ticked in advance, and refusing is as easy as accepting. A banner that fails any of these is a P1 finding.

## Marketing email (PECR reg. 22)

Marketing email to an individual needs their consent, except the **soft opt-in** (reg. 22(3)): the details were obtained in the course of a sale or negotiations for a sale; the marketing is for the sender's own similar products and services; and the person was given a simple means of refusing when the details were collected and in every later message. From 5 February 2026 a charity has a parallel opt-in for supporters (reg. 22(3A)) (checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/22). So a form where the owner may send offers carries an **unticked** box to refuse, and the owner keeps the refusals (the handout).

## Who we are (E-Commerce Regs 2002 reg. 6; Companies regs 2015)

Every business site makes these "easily, directly and permanently accessible": the business's name; the **geographic address** where it is established; contact details including an **email address**; a trade register and number if registered; a supervisory authority if the activity needs authorisation; a regulated profession's body, title and rules; a VAT number if registered for VAT; and prices that say whether they include tax and delivery (reg. 6, checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2002/2013/regulation/6). A contact form does not replace the email address. Case C-298/07 (CJEU, 16 October 2008) held that the email address must be given and a second rapid channel offered, which an online enquiry form can be: **unverified at source** on 2026-11-02, because EUR-Lex and curia.europa.eu returned no text to an automated fetch; read the judgment there before citing it.

A **company** also shows its registered name (reg. 24(2)), and the part of the UK it is registered in, its registered number and its registered office address (reg. 25) (checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/17/regulation/24 and https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/17/regulation/25). legislation.gov.uk notes reg. 25 is temporarily restricted by SI 2024/233; read that before writing the address line.

A sole trader who does not want a home address published has made an owner decision, and the accepted-risk record says plainly what it accepts: **not meeting reg. 6**. Whether another address (a business address, a training venue) is "the geographic address at which the service provider is established" is a legal question for an adviser; never present one as the fix.

## Reviews and ratings (DMCC Act 2024, Sch. 20 para. 13)

Since 6 April 2025 it is a banned practice to submit or commission a fake consumer review, to hide that a review was incentivised, or to publish reviews in a misleading way, including leaving out or removing negative reviews while publishing positive ones, or giving positive ones more prominence; a trader who publishes reviews takes reasonable and proportionate steps to prevent and remove fake ones (checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/schedule/20). So review stars on a business's own site are illegal in the UK if fake or filtered. Whether stars belong in its structured data is `kiss-seo`'s.

## Consumer contracts (flag only)

A site that takes bookings, deposits or payments from consumers is under the Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013: for a service contract, the cancellation period is 14 days after the contract is made (reg. 30, checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/30), with exceptions and conditions. **Flag it for an adviser; never draft the terms.**

**Prices.** Since 6 April 2025, an invitation to purchase that leaves out the **total price** omits material information, and the total includes the "fees, taxes, charges or other payments that the consumer will necessarily incur"; where it cannot be worked out in advance, the page says how it is calculated (DMCC Act 2024 s.230(2), (4), checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/section/230). So a price on the site includes every compulsory fee (a booking fee, a deposit that is not refunded) or says how it is worked out. Report a headline price that leaves one out as P1, and flag whether a page is an "invitation to purchase" at all for an adviser.

## Copyright and licences (CDPA 1988)

Copying a work, issuing copies or communicating it to the public without the owner's licence infringes copyright (s.16, checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/16). Every photo, illustration, font, icon set and quoted text the site did not make needs a licence: name its source, and give the attribution in the form the licence asks for. A photo of a client or their child needs the owner's record of their consent (the handout).

## Retention benchmarks

A notice says how long details are kept. Common anchors, for the owner to choose from:

- **Tax records:** a self-employed person keeps them at least 5 years after the 31 January submission deadline of the relevant tax year (checked 2026-11-02, https://www.gov.uk/self-employed-records/how-long-to-keep-your-records).
- **Contract claims:** 6 years from when the cause of action accrued (Limitation Act 1980 s.5, England and Wales, checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1980/58/section/5).
- **Personal injury:** 3 years from the injury or the date of knowledge, if later (s.11(4), checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1980/58/section/11). For someone under a disability, time runs from when that ends (s.28), and a child is under a disability (s.38(2)), so a claim for an injured child can come years later (checked 2026-11-02, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1980/58/section/28 and https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1980/58/section/38).

Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own limitation law, not checked here.
