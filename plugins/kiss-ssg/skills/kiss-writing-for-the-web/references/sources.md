# Sources

The rules in this skill come from published research and guidance. Where a number appears, the skill says how strong its evidence is (`SKILL.md` § Number guide).

## Evidence

- **Nielsen Norman Group (NN/g):** usability testing, eye-tracking and interaction research, and its UX writing guidance. Its classic study found concise copy improved measured usability by 58%, scannable layout by 47%, objective language by 27%, and all three together by 124%. These show the direction of the effect, not a conversion uplift to promise for a modern site. Also the source for reading levels (grade 6–8 for a general audience, 10–12 for experts), command labels of 2–4 words, link text, error messages, empty states, progressive disclosure, tooltips and notifications.
- **GOV.UK and the GOV.UK Design System:** reusable patterns tested across high-volume public services. The 25-word sentence review threshold, paragraphs of about five sentences, one question per page, hint text, specific error messages and next steps.
- **Baymard Institute:** large-scale ecommerce research (more than 30,000 manually assessed product-page UX scores). Product descriptions, specification sheets, returns information and reviews.
- **W3C / WCAG 2.2:** the normative source for accessibility, including labels or instructions for input and the accessible identification of errors.
- **Google Search Central:** people-first content, descriptive titles and headings, natural use of the words people search with, meaningful link text, and no preferred word count.
- **Microsoft Style Guide:** a warm, helpful, concise voice.
- **Content Design London:** length follows the user's need, and clarity can need more words.

## Adapted material

`ai-and-marketing-tells.md` adapts patterns from the **Humanizer** skill (https://github.com/blader/humanizer), which draws on Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing", maintained by WikiProject AI Cleanup. The patterns were cut to those that matter for web copy and rewritten for it; the ones that conflict with writing for the web (turning lists into prose, banning dashes) were left out.
